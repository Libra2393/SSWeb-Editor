Open a save

Drop a raw wiiking2.sav here or use Open. Dolphin keeps it unencrypted in its NAND folder:

  • Linux~/.local/share/dolphin-emu/Wii/title/00010000/<region>/data/
  • WindowsDocuments\Dolphin Emulator\Wii\title\00010000\<region>\data\
  • macOS~/Library/Application Support/Dolphin/Wii/title/00010000/<region>/data/

The last folder depends on your copy of the game:

  • NTSC-U534f5545SOUE
  • PAL534f5550SOUP
  • NTSC-J534f554aSOUJ
  • Korea534f554bSOUK

Close the game in Dolphin before saving over the file.

Skyward Sword HD saves (also wiiking2.sav, 122,400 bytes) open too. Get them with a save manager such as JKSV on a Switch, or from an emulator's save folder for title 01002DA013484000. Keep a backup of your save before editing.

Want it offline? Download SSWeb-Editor.html: the whole editor in one file. Double-click it to open it in your browser; it works without internet or installing anything.

    Before you continue (United States)

    • This is not legal advice.
    • The DMCA (17 U.S.C. § 1201) prohibits getting around encryption that controls access to copyrighted works, and distributing tools for doing so. Section 1201(f) allows some circumvention for interoperability. Moving your own save data to an emulator you own is commonly seen as falling under that, but we're not aware of a court ruling or Copyright Office exemption that specifically covers it. The Copyright Office's video game exemptions cover narrow cases (preservation by libraries and archives, and games whose authentication servers were shut down); none is aimed at save files.
    • Because of this, the editor does not include or download any Nintendo keys. You supply them, and they stay in this browser.
    • The editor only decrypts. It cannot re-encrypt, re-sign or create SD exports, and saving always writes the raw wiiking2.sav used by Dolphin.
    • Use it only with saves you made or have the right to use. Nintendo's terms of service may also restrict modifying saves.
